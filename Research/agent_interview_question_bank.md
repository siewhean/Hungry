# Hungry App User Profiling Pack (Journey Map Ready)

## Purpose
This document is a **user profiling + simulated interview response pack** for a non-delivery food discovery app.
It includes:
1. A targeted interview question set.
2. 50 agent profiles from different backgrounds.
3. Simulated first-person answers (as if each person is speaking from real usage).
4. Consolidated journey-map insights at the end.

---

## A. Core Interview Questions (Targeted)

### Consumer Questions (ask all consumers)
1. **Trigger moment:** “When exactly do you feel stuck deciding what to eat?”
2. **Decision blockers:** “What 2–3 factors most often stop you from choosing quickly?”
3. **Time pressure:** “What is the max wait (queue + prep) you’ll tolerate today?”
4. **Budget boundary:** “What price point makes you reject a dish instantly?”
5. **Trust signals:** “What makes you trust an app recommendation enough to walk there?”
6. **Failure memory:** “Tell me about a recent bad food-choice experience and what data was missing.”
7. **Feature wish:** “If you only get one feature, what saves your decision fastest?”

### Stall Owner Questions (ask all operators)
1. **Daily pain:** “What operational issue hurts you most every day?”
2. **Demand volatility:** “When do you over-prepare or run out?”
3. **Discovery fairness:** “What feels unfair in how platforms expose stalls?”
4. **Customer friction:** “What repeated customer confusion slows service?”
5. **Needed tool:** “What one app feature would improve your daily operations this month?”

## C. Consolidated Journey-Map Insights

### 1) What makes users happy
- Fast, confident decisions in under 30–60 seconds.
- Reliable “open now,” queue, and price data.
- Personalized shortlists by budget/time/dietary needs.
- Less walking and fewer failed trips.
- Fair discovery beyond hype.

### 2) Common consumer problems
- Decision paralysis during hunger + time pressure.
- Outdated opening hours and inconsistent price listings.
- Missing dietary/allergen/accessibility information.
- Too many taps and generic recommendations.
- Peak-hour unpredictability.

### 3) Common operator problems
- Demand volatility causing waste or stockouts.
- Queue bottlenecks and weak real-time communication.
- Discovery inequality for small/new stalls.
- Hard-to-use operator tools.
- Poor trust protection for compliance labels.

### 4) Features to prioritize (non-delivery)
1. Hungry-now quick mode (budget + wait + distance).
2. Data freshness trust layer (hours/price/queue timestamps).
3. Strict dietary safety modes (halal/vegan/allergen/diabetic).
4. Accessibility-aware discovery.
5. Operator one-tap status controls.
6. Demand prediction and off-peak nudging.
7. Fair, quality-weighted discovery ranking.

### 5) What users like from other apps
- Easy filtering and visual browsing.
- Recent real photos.
- Saved favorites and quick repeat choices.

### 6) What users dislike from other apps
- Stale information.
- Overly generic recommendations.
- Paid or hype-heavy ranking behavior.
- Long decision flows.

## D. How to Use This for a User Journey Map
Map each persona through stages:
1. **Trigger:** Hunger + context (time/budget/group).
2. **Search:** How they filter or browse.
3. **Evaluate:** Trust checks (queue/price/open/dietary).
4. **Decide:** Final stall/dish selection.
5. **Outcome:** Satisfaction or regret.
6. **Recovery:** What they do if first choice fails.

Capture emotions at each step: anxious → uncertain → confident (or frustrated).
