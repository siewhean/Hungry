# Hungry Brand Voice And Visual Rules

## Purpose

Keep launch assets aligned with the product's existing brand system and avoid drifting into generic food-tech or startup marketing.

## Voice Rules

### What Hungry should sound like
- Warm
- Direct
- Useful
- Local
- Grounded
- Appetite-led

### What Hungry should not sound like
- Corporate
- Overly clever
- Hype-heavy
- Venture-backed jargon
- Lifestyle-magazine vague
- Premium-only restaurant culture

## Writing Rules

- Start from the real problem: deciding what to eat
- Keep copy concise
- Favor sharp utility over brand poetry
- Use local language lightly and intentionally
- Prefer "dish", "stall", "hawker centre", "nearby", and "taste match" over generic dining terms
- Keep one clear CTA per asset

## Banned Copy Patterns

- "Discover amazing restaurants"
- "Build your food journey"
- "Unlock your cravings"
- "The future of food-tech"
- Fake urgency such as "last chance"
- Fake social proof such as "trusted by thousands"

## Visual System

### Primary colors
- Paper Warm: `#FFF7EE`
- Hungry Orange: `#E8531E`
- Deep Navy: `#1A2E44`
- Hawker Amber: `#F5A623`
- Autumn Green: `#4F7E17`
- Line: `#E7DED4`

### Typography
- Nunito for headings and body
- DM Mono for stats, prices, and structured labels

### Overall look
- Warm
- Clean
- Mobile-first
- Food photography carries emotional weight
- UI provides the proof

## Asset Mix Rules

- Product UI should make the claim believable
- Real food and hawker imagery should make the asset feel local and appetizing
- Maintain a balanced mix across the campaign
- Within a single asset, one side usually leads and the other supports

## Photography Rules

- Use real dish photography where possible
- Prefer local-food realism over polished ad fantasy
- Show texture, stall context, trays, counters, signage fragments, and lunch-rush atmosphere
- Avoid AI-generated food imagery presented as real menu proof

## Motion Rules

- Short-form video should hook in the first 3 seconds
- Silent viewing must still work
- Text overlays should be readable on small screens
- Motion should clarify the flow, not decorate it

## UI Usage Rules

- Show real product behavior, not abstract floating mockups
- Pull from actual app screens and design prototypes where possible
- Use Discover/Search, Stall Detail, and related screens as the main proof surfaces
- Avoid showing future-only features as if they are already shipped

## Claims Policy

- Only make claims traceable to repo docs, implemented screens, or clearly labeled roadmap items
- Never invent customer counts, testimonials, logos, or market traction
- If a feature is roadmap-only, label it as coming later or future-facing

## Channel Adaptation Rules

- Instagram: visual, save-worthy, still utility-led
- TikTok: fast contrast, strong hook, clear product difference
- Reddit / Telegram / Facebook: plain-spoken and discussion-ready
- LinkedIn: product insight and local-UX thesis, not viral founder theater

## Final Checklist

- Does this asset clearly explain the dish-first difference?
- Does it feel Singapore-local without becoming a caricature?
- Is the CTA singular and obvious?
- Are the visuals truthful to the product?
- Would this still make sense with audio muted?
