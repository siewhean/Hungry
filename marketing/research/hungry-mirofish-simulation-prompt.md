# Hungry MiroFish Simulation Prompt

Use this prompt in MiroFish together with the seed PDF.

```text
Simulate market reaction to Hungry, a dish-first food discovery app for Singapore, with a before-and-after comparison around marketing exposure.

Run this as a two-stage consumer-reaction simulation, grounded in the attached seed document.

Stage 1: Before marketing
- Simulate how target users react when they only hear the plain app idea without polished launch messaging.
- Measure immediate understanding, emotional response, perceived usefulness, skepticism, distinctiveness, and intent to try.

Stage 2: After marketing
- Then expose the same simulated audience to the current Hungry positioning and launch-style marketing materials described in the seed.
- Measure how reaction changes after they see the dish-first messaging, the hawker-centre framing, the practical utility claims, and the waitlist-oriented launch copy.

Target audience mix:
- Primary: Singapore office workers, students, and everyday local diners who frequently decide lunch nearby
- Secondary: tourists and expats in Singapore who want more confidence discovering local food
- Small secondary business segment: hawker stall owners who may care about dish-first discoverability

I want the report to answer these questions in a concrete way:
- How do people feel about Hungry before seeing marketing materials?
- How do people feel after seeing the current marketing materials?
- What do they like most about the app idea?
- What still feels confusing, weak, or unconvincing?
- Which claims feel credible and which feel generic?
- Which features drive the strongest intent to try?
- What objections stop people from joining the waitlist or trying the app?
- What should be improved in the product concept?
- What should be improved in the marketing materials?

Please structure the final report with:
1. Executive summary
2. Audience segment breakdown
3. Before-marketing reaction
4. After-marketing reaction
5. Key shifts from before to after
6. What users like
7. What users distrust or question
8. Product improvements
9. Marketing improvements
10. Suggested stronger positioning or copy angles

Important analysis requirements:
- Prioritize realistic, behaviorally plausible reactions over generic praise
- Highlight emotional tone such as curiosity, relief, confusion, skepticism, or indifference
- Separate feedback by audience segment where useful
- Call out whether Hungry sounds truly different from Google Maps, GrabFood, Burpple, or generic restaurant-discovery apps
- Include direct examples of likely user reactions, objections, or quote-style summaries from simulated agents
- End with a concise recommendation on whether the current launch messaging is strong enough for a waitlist test
```
