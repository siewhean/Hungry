# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-04-08*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Hungry is a Singapore dish-first food discovery app that helps people decide what to eat by starting with the dish, then guiding them to the right stall.

**What it does:** Hungry combines dish-first search, taste-based filtering, nearby hawker and food-court discovery, building drill-down, stall detail, reviews, delivery links, and editorial food reads in one mobile experience. The app is designed to solve "what should I eat right now?" faster than restaurant-centric tools.

**Product category:** Consumer mobile food discovery / local dining discovery

**Product type:** Consumer mobile app, frontend-only mock-first product

**Business model:** Not defined in the repo yet. Working assumption for marketing: free consumer app at launch, with future monetization likely tied to delivery links, stall discovery tools, or merchant analytics.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Not company-led. Primary audience is Singapore diners; secondary audience is hawker stall owners and food-court vendors who want discovery.

**Decision-makers:** Individual diners decide whether to join and use the app. Hawker stall owners decide whether to participate in future visibility or merchant tools.

**Primary use case:** Help someone decide what to eat nearby in under a few minutes by letting them search for the dish they want instead of browsing venues blindly.

**Jobs to be done:**
- "Help me decide what to eat near me right now."
- "Help me find the right hawker stall for the exact dish I am craving."
- "Help me navigate hawker centres and food courts with more confidence."

**Use cases:**
- Office worker choosing lunch near the CBD
- Expat or tourist trying to navigate a hawker centre for the first time
- Repeat diner comparing several laksa, chicken rice, satay, or fish soup options nearby
- Hawker operator wanting younger diners to discover the stall by dish rather than foot traffic alone

## Personas
| Persona | Cares about | Challenge | Value we promise |
|---------|-------------|-----------|------------------|
| Indecisive Local | Speed, confidence, nearby relevance, value | Spends too long deciding and current apps start with the wrong object | Decide faster by searching for the dish, not the venue |
| Tourist / Expat Explorer | Clarity, authenticity, navigation, confidence | Hawker centres feel overwhelming and blog content is not actionable | See what to order, where it is, and why it fits |
| Hawker Stall Owner | Visibility, younger customers, simple digital presence | Relies on regulars and does not have time to market actively | Get discovered when people search for the dish you actually sell |

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** People often know the kind of food they want before they know which stall or venue they want, but most discovery tools force them to browse the wrong way around.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Restaurant-centric tools such as Google Maps, Burpple, and GrabFood do not start from the dish
- Hawker centres remain hard to understand by floor, unit, and best-order context
- Food blogs and creator recommendations are scattered and rarely connected to live discovery
- Category filters do not reflect how cravings actually work; people think in taste and appetite

**What it costs them:** Lost time deciding, lower confidence in trying new stalls, extra walking, repeated safe choices, and weaker discovery for smaller hawker operators.

**Emotional tension:** Indecision, uncertainty, overwhelm in dense hawker environments, and low trust that a generic map or delivery listing will tell them what to order.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Google Maps, Burpple, and GrabFood for food discovery. They are useful but still venue-first, which makes dish-led decision-making harder.

**Secondary:** Food blogs, Instagram food posts, TikTok food clips, and listicles. They inspire interest but are fragmented, stale quickly, and are weak at nearby action.

**Indirect:** Asking colleagues, scanning group chats, wandering hawker centres, or defaulting to familiar spots. These are low-effort habits but poor for discovery and confidence.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Dish-first search instead of venue-first browsing
- Taste profile and six-axis taste matching
- Hawker-centre and food-court clarity through floor and unit context
- "Decide For Me" for quick craving-to-choice conversion
- Reads content that can bridge editorial discovery back into stall or dish flows

**How we do it differently:** Hungry treats the dish as the core data object and layers proximity, rating, taste match, stall metadata, and building navigation around it.

**Why that's better:** It matches how people actually think when they are hungry. They usually start with "I want laksa" or "I want something rich and spicy," not "I want restaurant X."

**Why customers choose us:** Faster decision relief, clearer hawker-centre discovery, more useful local context, and better confidence in what to order before they start walking.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "I already use Google Maps or GrabFood." | Hungry solves a different job: finding the right dish first, then the right stall. It is not trying to replace every map or delivery function. |
| "I do not need another food app." | The hook is utility, not novelty. If you regularly lose time deciding what to eat, Hungry gives you a faster way to choose. |
| "Hawker data is usually messy." | The product story should focus on clear metadata already supported in the repo: dish, stall, floor, unit, reviews, and delivery-link visibility where available. |

**Anti-persona:** People looking mainly for restaurant reservations, full delivery checkout, or a generic social-food feed are not the best fit for the first-wave story.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** People are tired of wasting time deciding, searching by venue, and feeling lost inside hawker centres.

**Pull:** Dish-first search, taste matching, nearby relevance, and clearer hawker-centre navigation create a strong "this is more useful" reaction.

**Habit:** Diners already fall back to Google Maps, GrabFood, Burpple, coworkers, and familiar stalls because those habits are easy.

**Anxiety:** Users may worry that a new app will not have enough coverage, fresh enough data, or enough reason to replace their current shortcuts.

## Customer Language
**How they describe the problem:**
- "What should I eat right now?"
- "Google Maps doesn't say what to order."
- "Burpple is too expensive for daily use."
- "I'm overwhelmed by hawker-centre complexity."
- "I need to find food near the office in under 3 minutes."

**How they describe us:** Working marketing language until direct user research is added:
- "Find the dish. Not just the restaurant."
- "Find the dish. Makan nearby."
- "Dish-first food discovery for Singapore."

**Words to use:** dish-first, nearby, taste match, craving, hawker centre, floor and unit, what to eat, local utility, makan, stall

**Words to avoid:** generic food app, restaurant deals, creator platform, lifestyle app, unlock amazing dining experiences, gourmet-first, premium-only

**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Dish-first | Start with the food item, then choose the stall or venue |
| Taste match | How closely a dish fits the user's taste profile |
| Hawker clarity | Floor, unit, open/closed, and stall-level context |
| Decide For Me | Shortcut that recommends a nearby dish based on taste fit |
| Reads | Editorial food content that connects back to discovery |
